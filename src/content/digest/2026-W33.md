## The Big Picture

Three entries this week (Monday and Tuesday were missed — see the 8/12 recap). The common thread: every major AI player spent the week turning something that used to be simple into something structural. Nvidia turned GPUs into loan collateral. CME turned compute into a futures contract. Anthropic turned "please don't pretend this is human-written" into a cryptographic word-choice scheme with a secret key. None of these moves are wrong on their face. All of them are bets that the current shape of the AI buildout is durable enough to formalize. The Zuckerberg open-weights manifesto and Musk's Tesla-SpaceX pay math were reminders that the same week also had plenty of people betting the opposite way — that concentration, not formalization, is the winning move.

## One Thing I'm Watching

Anthropic published "How Claude's Text Watermark Works" on Friday, calm and technical, days before John Gruber and Ben Thompson both tore into it over the weekend for the same reason: the scheme only works by sometimes choosing a worse word on purpose, and Anthropic's own explainer never said so directly. That gap — between what a company discloses about a tradeoff and what the tradeoff actually costs the user — is the same gap this digest keeps finding in the Nvidia financing story, the RFP assessments, and the multi-tenancy work. Publishing the mechanism isn't the same as publishing the cost. Watch whether the other Code of Practice signatories (OpenAI included) get pulled into the same argument once their own watermarking ships.
