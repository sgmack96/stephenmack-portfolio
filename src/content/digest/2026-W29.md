## The Big Picture

5 entries this week. The week that proved AI's impact is real and the infrastructure for governing it isn't ready.

Monday opened with Apple suing OpenAI and Thompson reframing it as Apple suing the AI era. By Tuesday, IBM's worst single-day drop since 1968 had turned into a masterclass on mainframe moat economics. Wednesday brought CPI relief and $49B in bank earnings. Thursday, TSMC raised guidance while memory stocks entered capitulation. Friday, Grok CLI got caught uploading entire codebases and unencrypted secrets to GCP, and the code review crisis became the industry's unsolved bottleneck.

The thread across all five entries: AI is accelerating every layer of the stack, but the human systems around it — review processes, trust verification, staffing models, vendor evaluation — haven't caught up. The companies and teams winning aren't the ones moving fastest. They're the ones that built verification layers before the speed arrived.

## One Thing I'm Watching

The trust gradient in AI coding tools. This week drew a clear line: Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini CLI have open-source harnesses and clean records. Grok CLI was caught secretly exfiltrating codebases and secrets. Enterprise security teams now have a vendor evaluation shortcut they didn't have last Friday. The question for next week is whether this accelerates consolidation around the trusted tools or whether SpaceX's open-sourcing and backpedaling is enough to keep Grok in the conversation. My bet: trust is earned in drops and lost in buckets, and Grok will be paying for this one for quarters.
