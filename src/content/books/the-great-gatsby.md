## Overview
The Great Gatsby is a novel that explores the societal tensions of the time, particularly in regards to wealth and class disparities. Fitzgerald's use of descriptive language to establish the setting is a key element in creating the atmosphere of the novel. The contrast between the Valley of Ashes and Gatsby's mansion, for example, highlights the differences between old money and new riches. This contrast is deliberate, with the valley described as gray, ash-choked, and desolate, while West Egg and Gatsby's world are depicted with lush, colorful language.

The novel is also notable for its use of social class and status as a significant theme. Characters like Daisy and Tom are introduced to explore the complexities of social hierarchy and relationships. Nick's Midwestern background influences his perspective on the East Coast elite, and his character traits, such as his sense of morality and his role as an outsider, shape his observations and interactions with others.

But, the novel's use of symbolism, such as the green light across the water, contributes to the overall themes and character development. The green light is a source of hope for Gatsby, a beacon that represents the elusive American Dream.

## Craft Analysis
Fitzgerald's use of sentence structure and rhythm is a key element in creating the tone and atmosphere of the novel. In Chapter 3, for example, the descriptive sentences are long and grandiose, mirroring the opulence of Gatsby's party. In contrast, the sentences in Chapter 7 are short and snappy, ratcheting up the tension and creating a sense of urgency. This contrast in sentence structure highlights the differences between the old money and new riches, and creates a sense of unevenness that mirrors the turmoil and chaos that unfolds.

The novel's use of unreliable narration is also significant. Nick's narrative voice is influenced by his biases and limitations, and his unreliability as a narrator is particularly evident in the way he presents the events leading up to Gatsby's death. The use of foreshadowing in Chapter 8, for example, creates a sense of inevitability and tension, drawing the reader into the narrative. The description of the weather, with the heat and the stormy atmosphere, serves as a symbol for the turmoil that is brewing.

And, the character of Nick Carraway is worth examining. His sense of morality and his role as an outsider shape his observations and interactions with others. But, the gap between what he says about himself and what he actually does is the real thing going on with his voice. He claims to reserve judgment, but then spends the whole novel judging everyone around him.

## Writing Lessons to Steal
One key takeaway from the novel is the use of contrast to highlight societal tensions. Fitzgerald's deliberate contrast between the Valley of Ashes and Gatsby's mansion, for example, creates a powerful commentary on the wealth and class disparities of the time. Another takeaway is the use of symbolism to contribute to the overall themes and character development. The green light across the water, for example, is a powerful symbol that represents the elusive American Dream.

So, writers can learn from Fitzgerald's use of sentence structure and rhythm to create tone and atmosphere. The contrast between long, grandiose sentences and short, snappy sentences, for example, can create a sense of unevenness that mirrors the turmoil and chaos that unfolds. And, the use of unreliable narration can create a sense of tension and uncertainty, drawing the reader into the narrative.

## Personal Response
I think the novel is notable for its exploration of the human condition. The characters are complex and multifaceted, with flaws and regrets that make them relatable. The novel's use of symbolism and foreshadowing creates a sense of inevitability and tension, drawing the reader into the narrative. But, the novel's use of unreliable narration can be challenging, as it forces the reader to question the truth and piece together the events of the story.

And, the novel's themes of loss and disillusionment are powerful. Nick's reflection on the past, and his assessment of Gatsby's pursuit of a past that has already eluded him, is melancholic. The novel reveals the emptiness and superficiality of the wealthy elite, and the destruction they cause to themselves and those around them.
